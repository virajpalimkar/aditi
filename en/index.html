Before you read

From her son

My mother once told me that every post she wrote had a deeper meaning hidden in it. Each one held some part of life: a problem she wanted me to see, or the way through it that she had found.

I did not always listen. I was not the ideal son. I misbehaved, and I did things I should not have done. Through all of it she carried everything alone: my father's sudden death in 2015, moving to new cities with me, the cancer that came four times from 2020, the chemotherapy, the surgeries, the long quiet days. She was a brave warrior, and she stood her ground.

She passed away in 2026. I went back through everything she had written on Facebook and gathered it here, translated from her Marathi. Above each post there is a short note on what was happening in her life at the time, so that you can read her words knowing what she was carrying when she wrote them. It was never only for me. So here it is, for everyone.

1 · For Viraj

What she felt for you, Viraj

Drawn only from her own posts, 2014–2026

You made her, as much as she made you. From 2 August 1994, when she went in for her C-section so calmly that her mother-in-law was surprised, she counted you as the great event of her life. In 2016 she wrote that watching you grow was the most wonderful thing that ever happened to her, "because it wasn't only your growth, it was mine." Your patience made her patient. Your listening made her a listener. "While I was shaping him, without knowing it, he went on shaping me." While she was shaping you, you were quietly shaping her.

Pride, in the person more than the marks. She was proud of the scholarships, Kota at fifteen, 98%, VNIT. She was prouder of who you were: the boy who told her "Aai, did I come here to study or to think about food?", and who bore the ragging in silence because he had given his father his word. From the time she was pregnant, her wish was to give her country "a good citizen". On your 30th birthday she wrote that she had done it. The quality she loved most in you was your calm: "You have a very calm head."

Then the roles reversed. After Baba died in 2015, you told her "Aai, I want you with me from the first day" and never let her live alone. By 2017 she wrote that you had become her father and she your daughter, and "you are the backbone of my mind". In 2019 she wrote that the boy who once held her finger now offered his for her to hold. She marvelled that at 25 you understood what most people never do: that she was a person first and a mother after, and that you could be a reason for her happiness but should not be the only one.

Through her illness, her friend. When stage-3 cancer was found in October 2020, a month after you started a new job, she wrote that you handled everything alone and patiently. You chose the hospital, stayed the whole day at every chemotherapy session and stayed steady through the complications. The lockdown gave back the time she had lost since you left home at fifteen, and she found in you "a good friend", someone who saw her as a person, not only as Aai. The iPad, and the microphone she kept refusing, told her you understood not what she wanted but what she needed.

Her luck. "The one thing about me that anyone could envy... is you." (2023) If there were a vow to get the same son in every birth, she would keep it in every birth (2024). She asked Swami Samarth to hold your hand and hers forever, and called him the grandfather who would look after you.

What she asked of you. She asked you to be less lazy, and to test your full strength once so the confidence would be there when you needed it (2017). She asked you to look after yourself a little (2019), and to care for a future partner the way you cared for her (2025). Once, gently, in 2021, she wished that someday you would read what she wrote and listen to what she read: "That will be a great day for me."

On 2 August 2026 she wrote her last birthday post for you: on that day in 1994 she became a mother, "and I brought a lifetime of boundless love down to earth, for myself."

This book is that day she was waiting for.
